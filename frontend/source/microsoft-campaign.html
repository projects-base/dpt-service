    The method

    Everything below exists to solve one sentence you said: "when I take a break it was gone." That is not a knowledge problem, it's a spacing problem, and it has a known fix.

    01

    Never zero

    Twenty-five minutes, every day, including the 15-hour ones. At 11:40 PM if that's what's left. A zero day costs about three days of re-warming; a 25-minute day costs nothing. You're protecting the chain, not the session.

    02

    Weekdays code, weekends design

    You never decide what to work on — deciding is where tired people lose. Monday to Friday is DSA. One weekend day is the long block: LLD through October and November, HLD through December.

    03

    Review at +3, +10, +30

    Every solved problem comes back three times. A review is four minutes, not a re-solve: read the title, state the approach out loud in under two minutes, write the core loop from memory. Only a failure sends it back to the start of the queue.

    04

    One line per problem, same day

    2026-09-21 | 3. Longest Substring | sliding-window | 22m | SOLVED-hint | shrink left only while the dup is still in the window — the last field is the whole point. It's what you re-read in December. "Solved it" is not an insight.

    05

    Two strikes, then a catch-up — never a restart

    Miss two days in a row and you owe one 90-minute weekend session. That is the entire penalty. You do not go back to week one. The restart instinct is what has been eating your months.

    06

    Small and retained beats large and forgotten

    Around 125 problems seen three times each, not 400 seen once. Blind 75 is the spine; AlgoMaster's pattern tracks map onto the weeks below. Adding a third list is procrastination wearing a productive costume.

    The sixteen weeks

    Problems are LeetCode links; ticking one is how the page knows what to quiz you on. Each week's challenge has a pass condition you can't argue your way around — that's deliberate.

    Boss fights

    Interviewing is a separate skill from problem-solving. Two hundred solved problems teach you nothing about thinking out loud while someone watches you type.

    Library

    One resource per track. When you're stuck at 11 PM, watch rather than read — but watch the specific thing you're stuck on, not a playlist.
